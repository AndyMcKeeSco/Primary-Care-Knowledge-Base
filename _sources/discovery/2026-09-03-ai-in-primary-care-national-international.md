# Source discovery: AI in primary care — national and international

## Scope

Bounded search for authoritative national and international sources on artificial intelligence in primary care, intended to support later work on `QUE-0004`, `QUE-0005`, `QUE-0006`, `QUE-0014`, `QUE-0015`, `QUE-0016` and `QUE-0017`. The search covered research-landscape reviews, reported adoption, evaluated implementation, administrative workflow, patient-facing uses, clinical safety, governance, regulation and adoption requirements. It excluded vendor promotion and did not treat discovery as evidence of benefit, safety or Scottish applicability.

Search date: 2026-09-03.

## Search vocabulary

- Technology: artificial intelligence, AI, machine learning, generative AI, large language model, LLM, large multi-modal model, ambient AI, ambient scribe, clinical documentation.
- Setting: primary care, general practice, family medicine, community health, ambulatory care.
- Use and outcome: implementation, adoption, evaluation, workflow, administrative burden, clinical decision support, patient self-service, safety, bias, privacy, governance, regulation, failure, limitation and discontinuation.
- Geography and authority: Scotland, UK, NHS, England, international, WHO, NICE, MHRA, CQC and HRA.

## Searches performed

- Repository-wide search across existing `SRC`, `EVD` and `QUE` records for AI, artificial intelligence, machine learning, generative AI and large language models; no existing AI-specific source record was found.
- Europe PMC title and full-text queries for AI plus primary care/general practice, including scoping/systematic reviews, implementation, safety, patient experience, and ambient documentation. Results were screened for direct setting relevance, publication type, open access, duplication and recency.
- Crossref title search for AI and primary-care scoping or systematic reviews, followed to DOI, PubMed and PubMed Central records.
- Direct authoritative-site searches and verification through WHO, NHS England, the AI and Digital Regulations Service, NICE and Scottish Government.
- Searches for historical versions, limitations, evaluation, failure and discontinuation were incorporated into research and implementation queries.
- The configured general web-search provider was unavailable. Scottish Government search returned broad/noisy results without a verified primary-care-specific AI publication. Healthcare Improvement Scotland and RCGP search routes attempted in this run returned 404, so those organisational routes remain incompletely covered.

### SRC-0035 — Artificial Intelligence and Primary Care Research: A Scoping Review

- **Authority and type:** peer-reviewed international scoping review; 11 published or grey-literature databases.
- **Access:** open full text inspected through PubMed Central.
- **Relevance:** direct baseline map of AI purposes, primary-care functions, intended users, conditions, geographies and methods.
- **Likely evidential value:** research maturity, evaluation gaps, end-user involvement and distribution of research effort.
- **Limitations:** search predates the generative-AI wave; not an adoption or effectiveness estimate.
- **Jurisdiction:** international; Scottish applicability requires separate assessment.
- **Recommended next action:** bounded extraction for research-maturity and evaluation-gap findings, paired with a newer review.

### SRC-0036 — Generative artificial intelligence in primary care: an online survey of UK general practitioners

- **Authority and type:** externally peer-reviewed UK clinician survey.
- **Access:** complete open article, methods, results table and limitations inspected.
- **Relevance:** direct evidence candidate on UK GPs' self-reported use of general-purpose generative AI and task categories.
- **Likely evidential value:** adoption, documentation, clinical-reasoning use, guidance gaps, privacy and bias concerns.
- **Limitations:** non-probability online sample and self-report; no separable Scottish estimate; use does not establish safety or benefit.
- **Jurisdiction:** UK-wide mixed jurisdiction.
- **Recommended next action:** high-priority extraction for `QUE-0014` and `QUE-0015`, with explicit sampling and transferability limits.

### SRC-0037 — Use of Ambient AI Scribes to Reduce Administrative Burden and Professional Burnout

- **Authority and type:** peer-reviewed multicentre United States quality-improvement study.
- **Access:** complete open article, intervention, outcomes, analysis, funding and limitations inspected.
- **Relevance:** evaluated implementation of ambient documentation in ambulatory care; approximately half of the analysed clinicians were primary-care practitioners.
- **Likely evidential value:** administrative burden, documentation workflow, clinician experience, patient attention and implementation design.
- **Limitations:** pre/post design without concurrent control, 30-day follow-up, voluntary participants, self-reported outcomes and one commercial platform.
- **Jurisdiction:** United States; Scottish transferability is low to medium for mechanism and low for numerical effects.
- **Recommended next action:** extraction only as a non-Scottish implementation study for `QUE-0006` and `QUE-0017`.

### SRC-0038 — NHS England guidance on AI-enabled ambient scribing products

- **Authority and type:** current NHS England national implementation guidance.
- **Access:** complete maintained page and implementation sections inspected; published 27 April 2025 and last updated 29 July 2026.
- **Relevance:** direct operational guidance on adopting ambient scribing products in health and care.
- **Likely evidential value:** product assurance, clinical safety, consent, information governance, workflow, procurement and monitoring requirements.
- **Limitations:** intended guidance, not evidence of adoption or outcomes; England-specific organisation and governance.
- **Jurisdiction:** England; Scottish requirements must be checked independently.
- **Recommended next action:** bounded extraction of implementation constraints for `QUE-0015` and `QUE-0017` after Scottish applicability review.

### SRC-0039 — WHO guidance on large multi-modal models for health

- **Authority and type:** international normative guidance from the World Health Organization.
- **Access:** authoritative publication page, scope, 98-page artefact identity and rights metadata inspected; open publication available.
- **Relevance:** general-purpose and generative-AI governance for health applications.
- **Likely evidential value:** ethical and governance principles, risk allocation and stakeholder responsibilities.
- **Limitations:** not primary-care-specific, not Scottish law or NHS policy, and not evaluated implementation evidence.
- **Jurisdiction:** international.
- **Recommended next action:** extract only general guardrails that can be mapped explicitly to Scottish legal and organisational sources.

### SRC-0040 — AI and Digital Regulations Service for health and social care

- **Authority and type:** dynamic regulatory navigation service curated by NICE, MHRA, CQC and HRA.
- **Access:** homepage and developer/adopter pathways accessible and inspected.
- **Relevance:** current route to regulatory, evaluation and adoption requirements for AI and digital technologies.
- **Likely evidential value:** source discovery for product classification, evidence standards, research, quality and adoption obligations.
- **Limitations:** dynamic gateway rather than one stable policy artefact; regulator remits and territorial applicability vary.
- **Jurisdiction:** UK and England depending on the linked regulator or requirement.
- **Recommended next action:** use as a gateway to original requirements; do not extract a single composite regulatory claim from the homepage.

## Duplicate and version groups

- WHO's 2021 *Ethics and governance of artificial intelligence for health* was verified as a broader predecessor/context source. `SRC-0039` was retained because it is more specific to current large multi-modal and generative models; the 2021 document remains a potential separate source if foundational principles require historical comparison.
- The 2024 UK GP survey and a 2025 qualitative UK GP study by overlapping authors are distinct methods. The survey was retained for breadth; the qualitative study remains a follow-on candidate for clinician attitudes and implementation context.
- NHS England ambient-scribing guidance is a maintained page. Its publication and update dates are recorded; later versions should be treated as revisions of the same source unless materially republished.

## Rejected or deferred candidates

- Supplier case studies and product webpages: rejected as primary evidence candidates because of commercial incentive, selective reporting and poor comparability; they may later help document product features only.
- Preprints superseded by peer-reviewed publications: rejected in favour of the final version.
- AI studies confined to hospital, emergency, imaging, laboratory or single-specialty settings without a primary-care population or transferable workflow: rejected as out of scope.
- Diagnostic-accuracy papers without implementation, workflow or primary-care relevance: deferred for a separate clinically bounded question.
- General NICE search results: not retained as one source; `SRC-0040` provides a more structured route to original regulatory and evaluation requirements.

## Access failures and gaps

- General web search was unavailable, reducing coverage of professional-body, grey-literature and implementation sources.
- No verified Scotland-specific national publication on AI use in general practice or community pharmacy was found in the bounded run.
- Healthcare Improvement Scotland and RCGP search endpoints tested returned 404; targeted replacement searches are still required.
- Patient-facing primary-care AI, community-pharmacy AI, failed implementations, health-inequality effects and long-term outcome evaluations remain underrepresented.
- The 2020 scoping review requires an updated synthesis to cover generative AI and ambient documentation.

## Coverage confidence and stopping reason

**Coverage confidence: medium.** The search covered six materially different and authoritative source types and verified complete identity and access for retained records. Confidence is not high because general web search and two professional/Scottish organisational routes were unavailable, and current Scottish primary-care implementation evidence was not found.

The search stopped after the principal categories—research map, UK adoption, evaluated workflow implementation, national implementation guidance, international governance and regulatory navigation—were represented, while additional results were duplicates, preprints, condition-specific, hospital-only or vendor-led.

No evidence, claim, observation, problem, hypothesis or opportunity record was created or changed. All retained sources require a separate bounded Evidence Extractor assignment before substantive findings are used.
