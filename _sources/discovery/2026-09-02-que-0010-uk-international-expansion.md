# Source discovery: QUE-0010 UK and international expansion

## Scope

Expanded search for evidence relevant to `QUE-0010` beyond Scotland. Tier 1 covered England, Wales, Northern Ireland and UK-wide studies; tier 2 covered comparable primary-care systems, prioritising Ireland, Denmark, Netherlands, Norway, Canada, Australia and New Zealand; tier 3 covered systematic reviews. Topics were contact reasons, repeat/frequent attendance, administrative or low-complexity demand, consultation complexity, community-pharmacy presenting reasons and first-contact resolution. Non-Scottish findings were eligible only as contextual evidence with explicit transferability limits.

## Retained sources

### SRC-0032 — UK frequent-attender consultation patterns

Large CPRD GOLD retrospective cohort study covering 845 UK practices and 2000–2019. It directly estimates the share of consultation activity attributable to the top 10% of users. Transferability is medium for concentration of workload but low for Scotland-specific percentages because the primary estimates are pooled and database geography is clustered.

### SRC-0033 — Danish reasons for encounter and follow-up

Cross-sectional registration study in a comparable GP-gatekeeping system, with 2008–2009 reasons for encounter and follow-up composition plus a 1993 comparison. Transferability is medium-low: the classification and care model are informative, but country, financing, staffing, coding and period differ from Scotland.

### SRC-0034 — Scottish community-pharmacy Minor Ailment Service

This Scottish source emerged from the wider community-pharmacy search. It directly reports why surveyed service users chose community pharmacy, their hypothetical alternative and one-week further-care self-report. It predates NHS Pharmacy First Scotland and cannot establish causal diversion.

## Extracted evidence

- `EVD-0005`: UK frequent-attender concentration and trend.
- `EVD-0006`: Danish reason-for-encounter and follow-up composition.
- `EVD-0007`: Scottish Minor Ailment Service users' reported reasons, alternatives and further care.

All remain drafts with `confidence: unknown` pending human review. No claim-bearing relationship was assessed.

## Rejected or contextual candidates

- European frequent-attender systematic reviews were retained as possible future synthesis sources but not extracted because the current run prioritised original datasets with transparent numerators and definitions.
- England Pharmacy First first-year material was not extracted because the identified record was a preprint and service design differs from Scotland.
- Ontario skin minor-ailment-service research was too condition-specific to represent community-pharmacy demand composition.
- France, Switzerland, Italy, China and Japan reason-for-encounter studies were lower priority than the Danish study because their system context or sampled population was less comparable.
- Studies of missed appointments, emergency-department use and condition-specific attendance were rejected as indirect for the bounded question.

## Coverage and remaining gaps

Coverage confidence is **medium** for UK frequent-attender concentration and for identifying at least one comparable-system reason-distribution study. It is **low** for current Scottish contact-reason volumes, explicit low-complexity classification, current Pharmacy First presenting-condition distribution and first-contact resolution.

The search stopped after three methodologically distinct, open and directly relevant sources were fully inspected and further results were mainly reviews, condition-specific studies, non-comparable systems or duplicates. Remaining work should prioritise current Scottish or UK nation-specific administrative datasets and evaluations before adding more overseas estimates.
