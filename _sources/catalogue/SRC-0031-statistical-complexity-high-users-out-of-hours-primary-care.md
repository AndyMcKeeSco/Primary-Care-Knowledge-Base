# Statistical complexity of reasons for encounter in high users of out of hours primary care

## Artefact

Peer-reviewed research article by Sarah Stegink, Alison M Elliott and Christopher Burton analysing anonymised 2011 NHS 24 contacts for adults during Scottish primary-care out-of-hours periods. The article examines concentration of contact among high users, reasons for encounter and statistical measures of reason-for-encounter complexity.

## Source type

Retrospective analysis of routinely collected national service data.

## Author / publisher

Sarah Stegink, Alison M Elliott and Christopher Burton; published in *BMC Health Services Research* by Springer Nature.

## Quality notes

Directly relevant Scottish evidence for repeat use, reasons for encounter and one formal concept of complexity in out-of-hours primary care. The national dataset is large and the paper defines exclusions and sensitivity analyses. It is limited to NHS 24 contacts in 2011, out-of-hours periods and call-handler-coded presenting reasons. The complexity measures are statistical diversity measures, not clinical-complexity ratings. The source does not establish that high-use contacts are low complexity, avoidable or suitable for automation.
