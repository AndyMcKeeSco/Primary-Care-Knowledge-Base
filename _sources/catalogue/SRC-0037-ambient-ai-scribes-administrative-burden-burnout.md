# Use of Ambient AI Scribes to Reduce Administrative Burden and Professional Burnout

## Artefact

Multicentre quality-improvement study evaluating 30 days of one ambient-AI documentation platform among ambulatory clinicians in six United States health systems; approximately half of the analysed clinicians were primary-care practitioners.

## Source type

Prospective pre-intervention/post-intervention quality-improvement evaluation.

## Author / publisher

Kendra D. Olson and colleagues; *JAMA Network Open*.

## Publication date

2025-10-01.

## Geographic origin

United States.

## Locator

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.34976
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12492056/

## Access date

2026-09-03. Full article, intervention, outcomes, statistical analysis, funding and limitations were inspected through PubMed Central.

## Rights / sensitivity

Open-access article reporting deidentified aggregate survey data. No clinical recordings, transcripts or participant-level data are stored here.

## Quality notes

Useful evaluated-process and outcome candidate for ambient documentation in ambulatory and primary care. It lacks a concurrent control group, has short follow-up, uses voluntary participants and self-reported outcomes, and evaluates one commercial platform. United States workflow, reimbursement, liability and data-governance conditions limit Scottish transferability. Vendor and institutional relationships require explicit review during extraction.
