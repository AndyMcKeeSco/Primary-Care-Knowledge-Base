# Reported nurse vacancies in responding Scottish general practices during 2021/22

## Finding

In Public Health Scotland's 2022 General Practice Workforce Survey, **26% of responding practices** reported nurse vacancies during 2021/22. The source reported that **10% of responding practices** had vacancies for Advanced Nurse Practitioners and **19%** had vacancies for general-practice or treatment-room nurses. Across nurses working from responding practices, the source reported an **11.7% vacancy rate**, defined as vacant nurse hours divided by total nurse hours.

These measures indicate reported unfilled nursing capacity among survey respondents; they do not establish why posts were vacant, how long vacancies lasted, their effect on services, or a national count of vacant posts.

## Source context

`SRC-0020` is Public Health Scotland's *General Practice Workforce Survey 2022*, an Experimental Statistics publication. The vacancy measures were collected through the GP Data Collection Tool and are presented as survey results, not as a complete administrative census of vacancies.

## Population and geography

Scottish general practices responding to the 2022 GP Data Collection Tool. The practice percentages concern responding practices, and the hours-based vacancy rate concerns nurses working from responding practices. The source does not present these bounded vacancy findings as estimates scaled to every Scottish practice.

## Method

The survey was sent to 911 practices and received 707 responses by the closing date. Public Health Scotland excluded incomplete returns, returns without individual staff data and returns with poor data quality, leaving 517 practices with usable survey-response data for the publication overall. The bounded vacancy section reports practice-level percentages and an hours-based vacancy rate; the report extract does not provide a separate denominator count for each vacancy percentage.

## Time period

Vacancies reported during **2021/22**, in the survey collected for the workforce position at 31 March 2022. The release was published on 29 November 2022.

## Applicability to Scotland

Directly applicable to the responding Scottish general practices represented in the survey. Applicability to all Scottish general practices is limited by non-response, exclusions and the absence of national scaling stated for these vacancy measures. It does not cover community pharmacy, out-of-hours services or the wider multidisciplinary primary-care workforce.

## Limitations

The report does not provide the exact number of practices answering each vacancy item in the bounded section, vacancy duration, reasons for vacancies, funded establishment assumptions, geographical distribution or service effects. Practice percentages overlap because a practice could report more than one nursing designation vacancy, so 10% and 19% must not be added. The 11.7% rate uses hours rather than posts or people and is limited to responding practices. The publication overall retained 517 usable practice responses from more than 900 Scottish practices and labels its figures as estimates; non-response and data-quality exclusions may affect representativeness. Vacancy measures alone do not establish actual workload, unmet demand, patient access or care quality.
