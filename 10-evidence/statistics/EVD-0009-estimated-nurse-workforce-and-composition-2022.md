# Estimated nurse workforce and composition in Scottish general practice at 31 March 2022

## Finding

Public Health Scotland estimated that **2,414 registered nurses**, representing **1,690 whole-time equivalents (WTE)**, worked in Scottish general practices at 31 March 2022. One nurse WTE was defined as 37 contracted hours per week. General Practice Nurses were the largest specified designation group, accounting for **63% of estimated nurse headcount** and **60% of estimated nurse WTE**; Advanced Nurse Practitioners and Nurse Specialists together accounted for **27% of estimated headcount** and **31% of estimated WTE**.

The estimated total nurse headcount was 51 lower than in 2019, a decrease reported as 2%, while estimated WTE was unchanged at 1,690. Across the longer published comparison, estimated headcount increased from 2,125 in 2013 to 2,414 in 2022 (14%), and estimated WTE increased from 1,420 to 1,690 (19%).

## Source context

`SRC-0020` is Public Health Scotland's *General Practice Workforce Survey 2022*, an Experimental Statistics publication. These findings describe estimated workforce size and designation mix; they do not establish workload, effective capacity, service availability, care quality or whether the reported mix met population need.

## Population and geography

Registered nurses working in Scottish general practices represented by the 2022 GP Data Collection Tool. The national estimates were scaled from usable responding-practice data and are reported for Scotland.

## Method

The GP Data Collection Tool was sent to 911 practices. Public Health Scotland received 707 responses by the closing date; after excluding 123 incomplete returns, 25 returns without individual staff data and 42 returns for poor data quality, 517 practices remained with usable survey data. Nurse headcount and WTE estimates were scaled by the share of the practice population represented by responding practices, with scaling calculated by NHS Board, designation, age group and sex. The report states that the specified designation percentages do not aggregate to all nurses working in general practice.

## Time period

The estimates are as at **31 March 2022**. Comparisons use prior survey reference dates: 31 March 2019, 31 August 2017 and 2015, and 31 January 2013. The release was published on 29 November 2022; the landing page records later revisions, but does not identify a revision to these bounded nurse measures.

## Applicability to Scotland

Directly applicable to the estimated registered-nurse workforce in Scottish general practices for the stated survey date, subject to the sampling, scaling and data-quality limitations. It does not cover the whole Scottish primary-care workforce, community pharmacy, primary-care out-of-hours staff, or multidisciplinary-team composition.

## Limitations

The estimates are based on 517 usable practice responses from more than 900 practices and are not exact census figures. Population-share scaling assumes responding practices adequately represent non-responding practices within the applied strata. Designation figures include only the specified nursing designations and do not sum to the total nursing workforce. The report states that its newly collected multidisciplinary-team section was excluded because of poor data quality and completeness, so this source cannot establish broader multidisciplinary skill mix. Comparisons across survey years require caution because collection methods and reference dates changed, particularly around the 2019 collection. Counts and designation shares do not measure workforce sufficiency, workload, deployable capacity, access or quality.
