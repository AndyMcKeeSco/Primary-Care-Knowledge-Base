# General and unspecified reasons were the largest Danish general-practice encounter group in 2009

## Finding

In 11,872 symptom- and disease-related GP contacts registered in the Central Denmark Region in 2008–2009, the ICPC general/unspecified chapter—including processes such as prescriptions and certificates—accounted for 23.8% of reasons for encounter. Musculoskeletal reasons accounted for 14.3%, respiratory 9.9%, psychological 8.6% and skin 8.3%. Additional problems beyond the main reason were discussed in 35% of GP consultations.

Across 13,348 GP and nurse contacts with episode status recorded or unknown, 49.9% were follow-up contacts, 39.8% new episodes and 10.3% unknown. Among nurse contacts, 76.1% were follow-ups. These categories describe the Danish study and do not identify low-complexity or avoidable work.

## Source context

`SRC-0033` compared a 2008–2009 Central Denmark Region contact-registration study with a similar 1993 survey. Danish GPs act as gatekeepers and almost all citizens are listed with a practice, but financing and service organisation differ from Scotland.

## Population and geography

Patients contacting participating general practices in the Central Denmark Region. Four hundred and four of 871 invited GPs participated; staff contacts were included for follow-up status but excluded from reason-for-encounter distributions.

## Method

Participating GPs registered all contacts on one randomly allocated day across a 12-month period. Reasons and diagnoses were recorded or coded using ICPC; researchers validated codes. The study extrapolated nurse activity for practices whose staff did not participate.

## Time period

December 2008 to December 2009, with comparison to 1993.

## Applicability to Scotland

Transferability is **medium-low**. Denmark has list-based GP gatekeeping and strong primary care, making the reason categories useful for hypothesis formation. Exact proportions should not be applied to Scotland because the country, financing, staffing, coding practices and period differ.

## Limitations

The GP response rate was 46.4%; women and less-senior GPs were somewhat overrepresented. Reasons were not collected for staff contacts, and some diagnoses were inferred from recorded reason and procedures. One registration day per GP and extrapolated nurse activity introduce sampling assumptions. Results are historical and non-Scottish.
