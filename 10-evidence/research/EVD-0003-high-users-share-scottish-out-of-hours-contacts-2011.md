# High users accounted for 15.1% of analysed Scottish out-of-hours contacts in 2011

## Finding

In the study's analysed 2011 NHS 24 out-of-hours dataset, 13,981 adults who made five or more contacts in the calendar year were designated high users. The authors reported that this group represented 2.4% of adults using the service and accounted for 120,395 (15.1%) of the 782,281 contacts retained after exclusions and duplicate removal.

## Source context

`SRC-0031` is a peer-reviewed retrospective analysis of anonymised contacts with NHS 24, described by the authors as the primary-care out-of-hours service for the whole of Scotland. The finding describes concentration of recorded contacts; it does not assess need, appropriateness, avoidability or outcome.

## Population and geography

Adults aged over 18 contacting NHS 24 during periods when normal GP services were closed across Scotland in 2011. The final analytic dataset contained 507,934 patients and 782,281 contacts.

## Method

The authors analysed all 2011 NHS 24 contacts, restricted the dataset to adults and out-of-hours periods, excluded contacts without a problem code and contacts related to pregnancy or childbirth, and removed duplicate contacts. They pre-specified five or more contacts in the year as the high-use threshold and also described subgroups of 5–10, 11–20, 21–30 and more than 30 contacts. The 2.4% share is reported by the authors and is not recalculated here from the final post-exclusion patient count.

## Time period

Calendar year 2011.

## Applicability to Scotland

Directly applicable to recorded adult NHS 24 primary-care out-of-hours use in Scotland for the study year. It is not directly applicable to in-hours general practice, community pharmacy, children, current service configuration or all primary-care contacts.

## Limitations

The finding uses a study-defined high-use threshold rather than a clinical category. It covers one calendar year and only out-of-hours NHS 24 contacts. Call-handler problem coding, exclusions and duplicate-removal rules affect the analysed denominator. The source does not show whether repeated contacts were clinically necessary, low complexity, preventable or resolvable through another role or channel. Service organisation and demand may have changed since 2011.
