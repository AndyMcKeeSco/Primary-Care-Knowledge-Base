# What share of primary-care contacts could be resolved at first contact by another role or channel?

## Question

What proportion of contacts currently reaching a GP in Scottish primary care could be safely and appropriately resolved at first contact by another role (for example pharmacist, nurse, physiotherapist) or through a different channel?

## Why it matters

Directing demand to the most appropriate first point of contact is central to Scottish primary-care reform (multidisciplinary teams, community pharmacy first). Quantifying the resolvable share indicates the ceiling on capacity that better routing could release.

## Decision enabled

Where to invest in triage, routing or skill-mix change, and which contact types are safe to redirect.

## Current knowledge

Not yet established. No extracted evidence characterises the redirectable share for any Scottish setting.

## Evidence gap

No findings link contact reasons to appropriate first-contact role or channel, or assess the safety of redirection.

## Proposed method

Extract evidence on contact reasons and existing role-substitution schemes from activity, workforce and evaluation sources; assess redirectability against safety constraints. Treat triage functions as requiring clinical scrutiny. No patient-level data.

## Priority rationale

High policy relevance and capacity leverage; depends on demand-composition evidence (`QUE-0010`).

## Dependencies

Depends on `QUE-0010`; relates to `QUE-0004`, persona `PER-0003` (multidisciplinary team member), and `CS-0002`.

## Answer/closure criteria

Closed when at least one contact type is evidenced as safely resolvable by an alternative role or channel, with an indication of its share of demand and the safety basis for redirection.
