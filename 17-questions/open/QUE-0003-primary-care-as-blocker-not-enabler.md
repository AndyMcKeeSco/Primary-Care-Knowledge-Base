# Where is primary care acting as a blocker rather than an enabler for patients?

## Question

At which points in patient journeys does Scottish primary care obstruct, delay or deflect a patient's legitimate need — acting as a gate or bottleneck — rather than enabling timely, appropriate care?

## Why it matters

Primary care is designed as the coordinating front door to the system. Where it blocks instead of enables (for example, appointment rationing, rigid routing, or access channels that exclude some groups), it can worsen outcomes and shift demand elsewhere. Naming these points focuses improvement on patient-facing harm.

## Decision enabled

Which access, triage or routing mechanisms to target for redesign, and where technology could remove a barrier without creating a new one.

## Current knowledge

Not yet established. No extracted evidence identifies blocking points; the distinction between appropriate gatekeeping and inappropriate blocking has not been characterised for any Scottish setting.

## Evidence gap

No findings separate clinically appropriate demand management from avoidable obstruction, nor identify which patient groups are most affected.

## Proposed method

Map patient journeys (`04-journeys`); extract evidence of access barriers and deflection from experience surveys and audit reports; distinguish intended demand management from unintended exclusion. Retain competing interpretations. No patient-level data.

## Priority rationale

High and patient-centred; directly serves the "outcomes" and "experience" objectives. Depends on journey modelling that is not yet present.

## Dependencies

Depends on the empty journeys layer (`04-journeys`). Relates to `QUE-0009` (access inequalities), personas `PER-0001`–`PER-0009`, and `CS-0001`.

## Answer/closure criteria

Closed when at least one blocking point is evidenced in a defined journey, with its affected population, the mechanism, and whether it reflects appropriate gatekeeping or avoidable obstruction stated with sources and limitations.
