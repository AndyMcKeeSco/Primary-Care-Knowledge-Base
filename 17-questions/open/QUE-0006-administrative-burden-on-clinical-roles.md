# What administrative work consumes clinical time with low clinical value?

## Question

Which administrative tasks are performed by GPs, nurses, pharmacists and other clinical staff in Scottish primary care that consume clinical time while adding little clinical value, and how much time do they take?

## Why it matters

Clinical time is the scarcest resource in primary care. Administrative work done by clinicians because of habit, system design or lack of alternatives is a direct capacity loss and a strong candidate for delegation or automation.

## Decision enabled

Which administrative tasks to redesign, delegate to other roles, or automate to release clinical capacity.

## Current knowledge

Not yet established. No task-level time evidence exists in the repository.

## Evidence gap

No extracted evidence quantifies clinical time spent on low-value administration by role and setting.

## Proposed method

Extract evidence from activity, workforce and evaluation sources; model the workflows in which the administration sits; classify tasks by clinical value and by who could appropriately perform them. No patient-level data.

## Priority rationale

High and tractable relative to broader efficiency questions; a strong feeder for `QUE-0004` (automation).

## Dependencies

Relates to `QUE-0004`, workforce sources, and care settings `CS-0001`/`CS-0002`.

## Answer/closure criteria

Closed when at least one administrative task is evidenced with its clinical role, time cost and a judgement on its clinical value, sources and limitations preserved.
