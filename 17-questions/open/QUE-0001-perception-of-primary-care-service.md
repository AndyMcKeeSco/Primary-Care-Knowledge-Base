# What is the perception of primary care service, both good and bad?

## Question

How do patients, carers and staff perceive Scottish primary care services — what is valued and works well, and what is experienced as poor, frustrating or failing — across general practice and community pharmacy?

## Why it matters

Perception shapes demand, trust, help-seeking behaviour and staff retention. Understanding both positive and negative perception is a prerequisite for judging where technology or AI would improve experience rather than erode it. It anchors the "experience" dimension of the model objective.

## Decision enabled

Whether a candidate opportunity targets a genuinely felt problem, and which experience dimensions (access, continuity, communication, respect, outcome) a change should protect or improve.

## Current knowledge

Not yet established in this repository; there are no production `EVD` or `OBS` records. Published Scottish sources exist (e.g. the Health and Care Experience Survey, `SRC-0022`) but have not been inspected or extracted.

## Evidence gap

No extracted findings distinguish measured satisfaction from reported experience, or positive from negative perception, for defined settings and populations. Staff perception is especially unrepresented.

## Proposed method

Extract bounded evidence from published national experience sources (starting with `SRC-0022`) and relevant audit/qualitative reports; record perception as observations with provenance. Separate patient-reported from staff-reported perception, and satisfaction scores from narrative experience. No patient-level or identifiable data.

## Priority rationale

High: broad, decision-relevant, and served by an accessible national data source, making it a tractable early extraction that grounds several downstream questions.

## Dependencies

Depends on verified access to experience sources. Relates to persona entities `PER-0001`–`PER-0009` and care settings `CS-0001`/`CS-0002` (referenced in prose pending formal edges).

## Answer/closure criteria

Closed when bounded, sourced findings describe valued and poorly-rated aspects of service for at least general practice and community pharmacy, with population, period and limitations preserved, and contradictions retained.
