# Which primary-care workflows vary most between practices, and does the variation affect outcomes or efficiency?

## Question

Which core workflows differ most between Scottish general practices (and community pharmacies), and is that variation associated with differences in outcomes, experience or efficiency?

## Why it matters

Unwarranted variation signals where standardisation or shared tooling could help, while warranted variation reflects legitimate local adaptation. Telling them apart prevents imposing uniformity where local context matters.

## Decision enabled

Where to standardise or share a workflow or tool, and where to preserve local discretion.

## Current knowledge

Not yet established. No workflow entities exist, so variation cannot yet be characterised.

## Evidence gap

No extracted evidence describes workflow variation or links it to outcome or efficiency differences.

## Proposed method

Model representative workflows; extract evidence of variation and its correlates from audit and evaluation sources; explicitly separate association from causation and warranted from unwarranted variation.

## Priority rationale

Medium: valuable but methodologically demanding and dependent on substantial prior workflow modelling.

## Dependencies

Depends on the empty workflows layer. Relates to `QUE-0002`, `CS-0001`.

## Answer/closure criteria

Closed when at least one workflow's variation is evidenced across practices with a qualified statement of any association with outcomes or efficiency, and the warranted/unwarranted distinction addressed.
