# Which patient groups face the greatest barriers to primary care, and why?

## Question

Which population groups in Scotland experience the greatest barriers to accessing primary care, through what mechanisms, and how do those barriers relate to deprivation, geography, digital exclusion and other factors?

## Why it matters

Any technology or AI change can widen or narrow inequality. Knowing who is least well served, and why, is essential to ensure improvements do not entrench the inverse-care law, and it underpins equity assessment of every opportunity.

## Decision enabled

Which groups an opportunity must be tested against for equity, and where targeted rather than universal change is warranted.

## Current knowledge

Not yet established here, though strong Scottish literature exists (for example Deep End research, `SRC-0030`, and multimorbidity–deprivation work, `SRC-0028`) that has not yet been extracted.

## Evidence gap

No extracted findings characterise access barriers by group and mechanism for current Scottish primary care.

## Proposed method

Extract evidence from experience surveys and inequalities-focused research; distinguish barrier mechanisms (availability, affordability of time, digital access, literacy, trust). Mark evidence origin and Scottish applicability. No patient-level data.

## Priority rationale

High: equity is a cross-cutting constraint on every other opportunity, and accessible Scottish sources make early extraction feasible.

## Dependencies

Relates to `QUE-0003`, `QUE-0005`, and personas `PER-0001`–`PER-0009`.

## Answer/closure criteria

Closed when at least one group's access barrier is evidenced with its mechanism, affected population and Scottish applicability, contradictions retained.
