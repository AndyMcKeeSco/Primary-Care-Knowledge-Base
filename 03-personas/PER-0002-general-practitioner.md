# General practitioner in Scottish general practice

## Role and context

A general practitioner working in Scottish general practice. Contractual intent and measured workforce are separate lenses: this persona does not assume that the 2018 contract model was implemented uniformly or that a national workforce survey describes every practice.

## Goals

Role-specific goals have not yet been extracted from the catalogued contract or workforce publication.

## Responsibilities

### Bounded responsibility map

The current model separates potential GP responsibilities into the following areas so that each can be researched without treating the job title as evidence:

| Area | Provisional responsibility | Current evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Undifferentiated presentations | Apply medical judgement where the person's condition, cause, urgency or appropriate route is not yet established. | **Intended-policy candidate.** `SRC-0012` should be extracted to verify the wording, scope and relationship to the contract's GP role. |
| Complex care | Provide medical generalist input where multiple conditions, medicines, risks, services or personal circumstances make care difficult to separate into one issue. | **Intended-policy candidate.** Contractual intent is not evidence of how often this occurs or who performs each activity locally. |
| Clinical decisions | Reach or contribute to decisions about assessment, investigation, treatment, prescribing, referral, review and escalation when these require GP judgement. | **Assumption to verify.** The consequence of error would be an over-broad model of GP decision rights and workload. |
| Continuity and coordination | Hold or contribute to a longitudinal view of care and coordinate with practice colleagues, multidisciplinary services and other care settings where GP input is required. | **Assumption to verify.** Continuity, coordination and administrative transfer must not be treated as the same responsibility. |
| Communication | Explain options, uncertainty, risk and follow-up, and involve the person using the service in decisions. | **Assumption to verify.** The model has not established how this responsibility is shared or supported. |
| Information and follow-up | Create or review clinically relevant records, results, correspondence and follow-up actions when GP accountability applies. | **Assumption to verify.** No specific inbox, results or document workflow is asserted. |
| Team contribution | Give medical input to colleagues and participate in safe delegation, escalation and hand-offs. | **Assumption to verify.** This does not imply that the GP manages every multidisciplinary-team member or retains work transferred under another service's governance. |
| Leadership and improvement | Contribute to clinical leadership, service quality, safety and improvement at practice or wider-system level. | **Intended-policy candidate.** `SRC-0012` requires bounded extraction, and local implementation and protected capacity require separate evidence. |
| Practice or employer duties | Undertake partnership, management, supervision, training, contractual or employer work where it forms part of the individual's actual role. | **Variable and unestablished.** These duties must not be assigned to every GP. |

This table is a research scaffold, not an `EVD` finding, clinical guidance or a universal job description. “GP responsibility” may mean professional accountability, work personally performed, oversight, a contractual duty or a locally assigned task; later workflow and decision records must state which meaning applies.

### Responsibility boundaries

- A GP being consulted does not by itself establish that the GP owns the whole workflow or every subsequent action.
- Delegated work, escalation and advice require explicit decision and interface modelling; they must not be represented as simple transfer of accountability.
- Responsibilities of GP partners, salaried GPs, sessional or locum GPs, trainees and supervisors may differ.
- Intended national policy must be tested against implementation, local governance and observed work before the persona can become `active`.

## Needs

Evidence is needed on information required for safe judgement, access to prior context, time and continuity, routes to multidisciplinary input, escalation mechanisms, and feedback after referrals or hand-offs. These are research needs, not established deficits.

## Pain points

Unknown from the evidence currently extracted. Workforce measures must not be converted into claims about workload, experience or capacity without a bounded finding and appropriate denominators.

## Capabilities

Medical-generalist judgement is the capability area to investigate from the intended contract model. Individual scope depends on professional competence, training, experience, role, governance and available support. This record does not authorise an activity or define clinical scope.

## Variation

Employment and partnership arrangements, career stage, sessional patterns, practice context, geography and local multidisciplinary provision may be material, but their definitions and distributions have not yet been extracted.

## Evidence

`SRC-0012` is the canonical catalogue record for the intended 2018 Scottish GMS contractual model, and `SRC-0020` is a release-specific Scottish general-practice workforce survey record. Neither has yet produced an `EVD` record. The responsibility map therefore distinguishes intended-policy candidates from explicit assumptions and variable duties; it must not be cited as a measured account of GP work. Confidence remains **low**.

## Not represented

- a named GP or a single employment model;
- proof of contract implementation;
- clinical guidance, authorisation, or a complete account of clinical scope, workload or decision rights;
- an assertion that GP work is consistent across practices, places or time.
